## 1. Executive summary

**Jomendez Inc** is a one-person consultancy that builds **complete lead-conversion systems** for local small businesses — smart websites + CRM + AI chat + automated follow-up + online booking + review automation, sold as one integrated system, not a stack of separate tools.

The brand positions the founder as a **Fractional CTO / Growth Systems Architect** for local businesses — someone who brings the rigor of large-tech engineering to small companies *without the overhead*. The pitch is explicitly **not** "web designer" and **not** "marketing agency": the differentiator is the operational system **behind** the website that captures, follows up with, and converts leads — automatically.

Audience: small-business owners across home services, health & wellness, real estate, and other local service categories — with a strong **bilingual (English / Spanish)** offering that opens up the US Hispanic-owned-SMB market.

## 9. Brand voice & tone

- **Clear, premium, practical, confident.**
- **Business-focused, non-technical, direct.**
- Translates technical experience into outcomes the owner actually feels (more booked appointments, faster follow-up, less manual work).
- Avoids jargon — when a technical term appears it gets a plain-English gloss in the same sentence.
- Lightly aspirational, never hypey. No "guaranteed leads" / "guaranteed revenue" — the site is careful to avoid promises that smell salesy.
- Slight personal warmth — the founder shows up in copy ("I", not just "we").

**Spanish register:** warm **tú** (informal), matching the founder-to-owner intimacy of the English. Tier names localized: **Inicial / Crecimiento / Pro / Personalizado**. "Tech Stack" is rendered as **Herramientas** — avoiding jargon for the audience.

### Phrases the brand uses (good for ad creative & meta descriptions)

- "Smart websites that help you capture and convert more leads"
- "Follow up with every lead automatically"
- "Turn website visitors into booked appointments"
- "Stop losing leads because of slow follow-up"
- "A complete digital system for your business, not just a website"
- "Simple for your team. Powerful behind the scenes."
- "Built for small businesses that need more than a pretty website"

### Phrases to avoid

- "Guaranteed leads" / "guaranteed revenue" / "guaranteed ranking"
- Heavy "Big Tech" prestige flexing — the audience doesn't connect to Amazon-the-brand; they connect to *what that experience does for them*
- Engineering jargon ("enterprise-scale", "microservices", "high-performance environments") without an immediate plain-English translation

## 15. Content gaps & opportunities

Today the site has 4 marketing pages. The biggest SEO levers from here are *content depth and breadth*. Recommended additions, in priority order:

1. **Industry landing pages** — one per priority industry (med spa, dental, HVAC, plumber, real estate, wellness clinic, coach/consultant). Each is its own primary-keyword target with industry-specific testimonials and outcomes. **Largest ROI lever.**
2. **Location landing pages** — Miami, Hialeah, Doral, Kendall, Orlando, eventually national Hispanic metros. Pair with bilingual variants.
3. **Comparison pages** — *Jomendez vs. a web designer*, *vs. a marketing agency*, *vs. doing it yourself (Wix/Squarespace)*, *vs. GoHighLevel agencies*.
4. **Educational blog posts** — answering the long-tail questions in §12.3. Strong People-Also-Ask alignment. Goal: rank for informational intent that funnels into commercial.
5. **Case studies / testimonials** — currently absent. Even 3–5 short, specific case studies dramatically lift trust and on-page conversion. Worth treating as a content priority once first delivered clients give consent.
6. **Bilingual versions** of each new content piece — same effort, double the keyword surface.
7. **Resource / glossary pages** — "What is a lead conversion system?", "What is a fractional CTO?", "What is missed-call text-back?". Cheap to write, great for featured-snippet and AEO (answer-engine optimization).

## 19. Notes for OTTO / SEO tooling

Things OTTO (or any other SEO/AEO tool) should know about the current state of the site:

- **Marketing pages eligible for indexing:** `/`, `/free-audit`, `/strategy-call`, `/contact`. All listed in the sitemap, all return 200 with self-referencing canonicals.
- **Sitemap:** `https://jomendez.io/sitemap.xml` (4 URLs).
- **Robots:** allows public crawl; disallows `/admin` and `/audit/` (private flows). Other LLM crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, etc.) **are not blocked** from public content.
- **Languages:** English + Spanish. Same URLs serve both — language is a client-side preference (no `/es` URL variant). For tools that want hreflang, the right read is "single URL, multiple languages, browser/preference resolved client-side."
- **Per-page meta** (title, description, canonical, OG, Twitter) is correct and route-specific in raw HTML.
- **Analytics:** Google Analytics 4 is live (`G-1YBS3MMF9M`). Enhanced Measurement handles SPA navigations.
- **OTTO is already loaded** site-wide as of the install of the SearchAtlas dynamic-optimization loader.
- **Two free conversion offers exist** that OTTO and ads should optimize for:
  - The **free instant audit** at `/free-audit` (no friction)
  - The **free 20-minute strategy call** at `/strategy-call` (higher intent)
- **Pricing surface:** `/#pricing` deep-links to the pricing section.

### What's worth leaning into

- Long-tail informational content (the §12.3 list) is genuinely underserved on the site today.
- Industry-specific landing pages and the bilingual track are the two biggest growth levers.
- Local-pack ranking in Miami and select Hispanic metros is achievable given the bilingual angle.

### Constraints / things not to do

- Don't use the word "guaranteed" anywhere — leads, ranking, revenue, returns. Off-brand and risk-flag.
- Don't pivot the brand back toward "web designer" or "marketing agency" framing. The differentiation is in the operational *system*.
- Don't republish the founder's home/private address. Schema is intentionally `Organization` without a street address.
- Avoid heavy "Big Tech" prestige flexing without immediately tying it to a benefit the small-business owner cares about.
